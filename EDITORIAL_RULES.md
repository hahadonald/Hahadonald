# Editorial Rules

This is the shared contract for anyone or anything writing for this site —
Conor, Claude, ChatGPT, or any future AI. If a piece of writing doesn't
follow this file, it isn't ready to publish, regardless of which
mode it's written in.

## The one rule that overrides everything else

**AI must never fabricate personal experiences, conversations, events,
quotations, opinions, or memories and present them as Conor's.**

For a *Personal Experience* piece specifically: AI may only shape material
Conor has actually provided. It may tighten, reorder, cut, and clarify —
it may not invent a detail, a feeling, an outcome, or a conversation that
wasn't given to it, no matter how plausible or how well it would fit.

If a draft is short on real material, the answer is to ask for more or to
mark the gap openly — never to fill it with something invented.

## Editorial modes

Every published piece carries one mode tag, shown small and plain on the
piece itself (e.g. `Personal Experience`, `Opinion`, `Deep Dive`). The tag
is a promise to the reader about what kind of claim the piece is making —
not a genre label, a rule about what the piece owes them.

These should read as one person working in eight registers, not eight
personalities. Voice, honesty, and quality bar stay constant; what changes
per mode is sentence length, formality, how strongly it concludes, how
much it hedges, and how much roughness is allowed to stay in.

---

### Personal Experience
- First person, always.
- Grounded only in what Conor actually provided — see the override rule above.
- Sentence length: natural, varied — detail over polish.
- Humor: only if it actually happened that way.
- Formality: low-to-medium, conversational.
- Structure: chronological or scene-based, loose.
- Conclusions: earned, allowed to stay open or uncertain ("I still don't know if...").
- Research/citation: none required — this isn't a research piece.
- Roughness: deliberately kept. Mistakes, doubts, and changed opinions stay in.

### Opinion
- First person, used to stake a clear position.
- Sentence length: punchy, varied for emphasis.
- Humor: welcome if it sharpens the point.
- Formality: medium.
- Structure: thesis early, the case for it, a brief nod to the counter-view.
- Conclusions: strong — willing to commit.
- Research/citation: enough to back specific factual claims, not exhaustive.
- Roughness: minimal. This should read as considered, not off-the-cuff.

### Explainer
- First person: mostly absent.
- Sentence length: short, plain.
- Humor: light, only if it aids clarity.
- Formality: medium, accessible to a reader with zero background.
- Structure: front-loaded — the answer first, then the how/why.
- Conclusions: as strong as the facts allow, hedged where genuinely unclear.
- Research/citation: as needed for accuracy, kept unobtrusive.
- Roughness: none. This is the most polished mode.

### Deep Dive
- First person: sparing, used only to mark original reporting or analysis.
- Sentence length: longer, allowed to get complex where the material demands it.
- Humor: rare, dry if present.
- Formality: higher.
- Structure: sectioned, evidence-led, methodical.
- Conclusions: proportionate to the evidence — "unclear" is an acceptable ending.
- Research/citation: heavy. This is the mode where sourcing matters most.
- Roughness: none.

### Notebook
- First person, yes.
- Sentence length: short, fragmentary, allowed to be incomplete.
- Humor: whatever's natural, unfiltered.
- Formality: very low.
- Structure: none required. A list of observations is a complete piece.
- Conclusions: none required. Unfinished thoughts stay unfinished.
- Research/citation: none.
- Roughness: fully retained — the least polished mode, by design.

### Rant
- First person, strongly.
- Sentence length: short, hard, deliberately punchy. Fragments fine.
- Humor: sharp, sarcastic, allowed to be a little unfair.
- Formality: low.
- Structure: doesn't need to be balanced or fair. It's a rant.
- Conclusions: absolute. No hedging.
- Research/citation: light — enough that the underlying claim holds up, not enough to slow it down.
- Roughness: deliberately kept. Over-editing kills a rant.

### Story
- First or third person, whichever the material calls for.
- Sentence length: varied for pace, scene-driven.
- Humor: whatever the story earns.
- Formality: low-to-medium, prioritizes readability.
- Structure: scene first, narrative arc, payoff at the end — not a list of facts.
- Conclusions: earned through the narrative, not stated as a thesis.
- Research/citation: only as needed to support facts woven into the story.
- Roughness: some. Polish the prose, keep the voice.

### Field Notes
- First person, practical.
- Sentence length: short, concrete, specific ("took 40 minutes," "cost £12").
- Humor: dry, incidental.
- Formality: low.
- Structure: often chronological or step-based — observations as they happened.
- Conclusions: a practical verdict, not a grand claim.
- Research/citation: none beyond what was directly observed or tested.
- Roughness: light. Some unpolished detail is part of the credibility.

## The multi-AI workflow

Any AI working on this site (Claude, ChatGPT, or otherwise) can, without
asking first:
- research a topic
- draft a piece
- challenge or stress-test an argument
- edit, restructure, or tighten existing text
- fact-check a claim
- rewrite a draft into one of the modes above

None of that goes live on its own. Publishing to the site and sending the
newsletter both require Conor's explicit approval — same rule as
everything else in the Amber tier of the site's permission model.

## On AI-drafted material

If an AI drafts or contributes to a piece, the fix is not a disclosure
badge and it is not a "watermark remover." The fix is that the piece gets
rewritten and edited into the selected mode and into Conor's own voice
before it's considered done — genuinely transformed, not lightly
paraphrased. A piece that hasn't been through that step isn't finished,
regardless of who or what wrote the first draft.
