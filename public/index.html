Exhibit 001Sept 2019

The Sharpie Amendment

During a briefing on Hurricane Dorian, a National Weather Service map appeared to have been altered with a black marker to extend the storm's projected path into Alabama — a state the official forecast hadn't included. Nobody in the room ever quite explained who held the pen. The internet gave it a name within the hour: Sharpiegate.

Source: NOAA, AP, and most major outlets, September 2019.

"My favorite part isn't the map — it's that an entire federal agency had to publicly disagree with a Sharpie."— curator's take (swap this for your own line)

Exhibit 002Apr 2020

The Light Bulb Idea

At a White House coronavirus briefing, the idea of getting disinfectant or UV light "inside the body" to fight the virus was floated out loud as something worth looking into. Poison control hotlines reported a jump in calls the next day. The idea was walked back almost immediately — but not before becoming a permanent case study in what not to say from a podium during a pandemic.

Source: White House briefing transcript, April 23, 2020; CDC and poison control statements that followed.

Exhibit 003Aug 2017

Eclipse, But Make It Ill-Advised

Millions of Americans dutifully wore their paper glasses for the August 2017 total solar eclipse. One person, watching from the White House balcony, looked straight up at the sun without them — caught on camera, forever, while an aide can be heard off-screen saying "don't look."

Source: widely captured on video and reported at the time, August 21, 2017.

"In fairness, everyone wants to look directly at the thing everyone told them not to. Most people just don't."— curator's take (swap this for your own line)

Exhibit 004May 2017

Covfefe

A late-night tweet ended mid-sentence with the word "covfefe" — not a typo anyone could parse, not a word in any language, never explained. It was deleted hours later, but not before earning its own Wikipedia entry and a press secretary joke that "a small group" of people understood exactly what it meant.

Source: the tweet itself, May 31, 2017 (since deleted).

Source: SiriusXM interview, May 1, 2017, widely covered at the time.

Exhibit 0062019 – ongoing

At various rallies since 2019, wind turbines have been blamed for killing birds, crashing property values, and — in a claim with no basis in science — causing cancer "if you're near it." Wind energy researchers issued the same correction more than once, to no evident effect on the material.

Source: multiple rally speeches, 2019–2020; fact-checked by PolitiFact and Reuters, among others.

"Somewhere, a wind turbine is very confused about its diagnosis."— curator's take (swap this for your own line)

Exhibit 007Sept 2017

Welcome to Nambia

Addressing African leaders at a UN luncheon, a speech referred to the health system of "Nambia" — a country that does not exist, likely a slip involving Zambia, Namibia, and Gambia. The White House later said it was a typo in the prepared text. The room's reaction, per reporters present, was a mix of confusion and polite silence.

Source: UN General Assembly transcript and pool reports, September 20, 2017.

Exhibit 010Feb 2017

Frederick Douglass, Recognized More and More

At a Black History Month listening session, Frederick Douglass was described as "an example of somebody who's done an amazing job and is being recognized more and more." Douglass died in 1895. Exhibit 011Jan 2017

The Biggest Crowd, Ever

The inauguration crowd was described as the largest ever assembled for the event. Side-by-side aerial photos comparing it to the 2009 inauguration said otherwise, and the National Park Service's own crowd estimates backed the photos. The press secretary defended the claim from the podium; a senior advisor later described the defense as offering "alternative facts," a phrase that outlived the argument itself.

Source: National Park Service imagery and multiple news outlets, January 21–22, 2017.

"The photos didn't lie. Everything else in the room did its best to."— curator's take (swap this for your own line)
