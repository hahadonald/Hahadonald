## OPINION

**Why "Rocket Man" Was Never Really About North Korea**

Nicknames aimed at foreign leaders get read as diplomacy — a signal sent
outward, to Pyongyang, to Kim Jong Un himself. That's the wrong audience.
"Rocket Man" wasn't built to persuade a dictator; a nickname has never
talked anyone out of a missile program. It was built for the room back
home — a headline-sized way to look tough without doing the much slower,
much less quotable work of actual negotiation.

That's the tell with a lot of the foreign-policy one-liners in this
dossier: they read as strength right up until you ask what changed
afterward. North Korea's missile program didn't stop. The nickname
outlived the tension it was supposedly managing by years. If the goal
had been Pyongyang, that would be a failure. If the goal was Tuesday's
news cycle, it worked perfectly.

The counter-argument is worth taking seriously, though: unconventional
rhetoric was also credited, rightly or not, by some foreign policy
figures with helping bring North Korea to the table for the 2018 summits
at all — a break from decades of quieter approaches that hadn't produced
much either. Whether that's cause and effect or coincidence is a real
argument, not a settled one.

## RANT

**The Paper Towels**

No. Not "context matters," not "it was well-intentioned," not "he was
trying to connect with the crowd." A basketball move. At a supply
distribution site. For people who'd just lost their roofs. You do not
need a communications degree to know that throwing anything, underhand
or not, at a disaster survivor reads as a joke you weren't invited to.
The rolls landed fine. Everything else about it landed exactly as badly
as it looks in the footage, which — eight years on — is still the first
thing that comes up when you mention that trip at all. That's not an
accident of framing. That's just what happened, on camera, in front of
everyone.

## PERSONAL EXPERIENCE — template only, not a demo

Per EDITORIAL_RULES.md, this mode can only be shaped from material you've
actually given me — I'm not writing a fake one just to show the format.
Here's the empty structure instead; if you ever hand me real material
for a piece like this, this is the shape it'd take:

> **[Title]**
>
> [Opening scene or moment — first person, specific, something that
> actually happened]
>
> [What you expected going in, if relevant]
>
> [What actually happened — including the parts that didn't go as
> planned]
>
> [Where you landed on it, allowed to be unresolved]

No content goes in those brackets unless you put it there.
